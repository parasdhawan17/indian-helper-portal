# Hiring a Domestic Helper in Singapore
## Knowledge Document for a Helper–Employer Matching Portal

**Prepared:** 26 October 2026
**Purpose:** Understand the real hiring process, the legal rules, what employers and helpers actually look for in Facebook groups and on existing portals, and what that means for building a portal where helpers post profiles and employers search with filters.
**Scope:** Singapore only. Foreign domestic workers are called **Migrant Domestic Workers (MDWs)** by MOM, and **Foreign Domestic Workers (FDWs)** in older documents. The public usually says “helper” or “maid”.

> This is research, not legal advice. MOM rules change, and the licensing question in Section 12 decides whether this business can operate as designed. Get a Singapore employment-agency lawyer to confirm that section before you charge money or launch.

---

## 1. Executive summary

1. **This is a large, recurring market.** There were **308,700 MDWs in Singapore as of June 2025**, up from 247,400 in December 2020 — growth of about 3% a year since 2011, driven by ageing and dual-income families with less extended-family support. That is more than 1 in 5 households. A parliamentary update in 2026 put the 2025 figure at 316,900.
2. **There are only two legal hiring routes:** hire **directly** yourself, or hire through a **MOM-licensed Employment Agency (EA)**. Engaging an *unlicensed* EA is itself an offence.
3. **Facebook is the de facto direct-hire marketplace today** — mainly groups like *Direct Hire Transfer Singapore Maid / Domestic Helper* and *MDW in Singapore* — because it is free and full of transfer helpers. It is also unstructured: no reliable filters, no verification, no status (is she still available?), public phone numbers, and agents and scammers mixed in with genuine posts.
4. **What people match on is predictable and can be structured.** Employers filter first on: availability / transfer status, nationality and language, main job (infant care, childcare, eldercare, cooking, general housework), experience in Singapore, expected salary, and rest days. Helpers filter first on: salary, rest days, family size and workload, own sleeping space, food, phone use, and location.
5. **Transfer helpers are the core of the Facebook market.** They are already in Singapore, can be met in person, can usually start in about 1–3 weeks, and command higher salaries. A fresh helper from overseas takes roughly 4–8 weeks and involves more steps (IPA, Settling-In Programme, arrival medical).
6. **The portal idea already exists in several forms** — NetMaid (agency listings, 28,000+ profiles), HelperChoice, HelperPlace, Anisya, Searchmaid Direct, EmployHelpers. None of them owns the Facebook-group experience in a clean, verified, mobile-first way. That gap is real — but so is the reason some have paused or stayed small: trust, supply quality, and licensing.
7. **The single biggest risk is not technology. It is the Employment Agencies Act.** Running an employment agency without a licence carries a fine of up to **S$80,000, jail up to 2 years, or both**. Activities that can count as EA activity include collecting resumes to help people find jobs, helping to place a jobseeker with an employer, and submitting Work Permit applications for others. Platforms like EmployHelpers explicitly position themselves as *“an internet platform, not an Employment Agency, and we do not hold an EA licence”* — and they carefully avoid placement and paperwork. Your portal must be designed around that line from day one, or it must get a licence, or partner with a licensed EA.
8. **Money in the current market:** employers pay agencies roughly **S$1,000–S$3,000** in service/placement-related fees for a conventional hire. Direct-hire platforms undercut that sharply — EmployHelpers, for example, markets a **S$39** employer fee and a DIY total of about S$689 versus S$2,850–S$3,450 through an agency (both totals include the same mandatory MOM, insurance and medical costs). Helpers must **never** be charged more than 1 month’s salary per year of contract, capped at 2 months, and only after placement — and the cleanest direct-hire platforms charge helpers nothing.

| EA | Employment Agency licensed by MOM |
| IPA | In-Principle Approval — MOM’s approval letter before the helper can enter Singapore / before a transfer is completed. It states salary, rest days and employer name, in English and the helper’s native language |

## 3. The legal frame — who can hire, and who can be hired

### 3.1 Helper eligibility (MOM)

A helper must meet **all** of these to get a Work Permit:

- **Female**
- **Age 23 to below 50** at Work Permit application. Helpers aged 50+ can only *renew*, up to age 60.
- **From an MOM-approved source country or region:** Bangladesh, Cambodia, Hong Kong, India, Indonesia, Macau, Malaysia, Myanmar, Philippines, South Korea, Sri Lanka, Taiwan, Thailand.
- **Minimum 8 years of formal education** with a recognised certificate.
- **Not related to the employer.**
- **Must work only for that employer, only at the employer’s NRIC-registered address**, and live there.
- **Must not be in Singapore when the application is made** (Malaysians excepted) — she can only enter after IPA is issued and the S$5,000 security bond is bought.
- **First-time helpers must attend the SIP** after arrival — MOM states this in the IPA letter.
- Must pass the **pre-employment medical** (see 5.3).

In practice the market concentrates in four nationalities — **Filipino, Indonesian, Myanmar and Indian** — with Sri Lankan helpers also common. Nationality matters commercially because language, cooking familiarity, religion/diet, and source-country salary floors differ.

### 3.2 Employer eligibility (MOM)

The employer must:

- Be **21 or older**
- **Not be an undischarged bankrupt**
- Have the **mental capacity** to understand and carry out employer responsibilities
- Have the **financial ability** to hire, maintain and house a helper in acceptable accommodation. MOM assesses income — there is no single published income threshold that guarantees approval. Married couples can combine income; a **Joint Income Scheme** (combine with one co-residing family member other than a spouse, if you are a citizen/PR and earn more than that family member) and a **Sponsorship Scheme** exist for cases like elderly employers.
- If hiring for the **first time**, complete the **EOP at least 2 working days before** submitting the Work Permit application. Online EOP costs about **S$29–S$36** (classroom S$35–S$76) and takes about 2–3 hours.
- Singapore citizens, PRs, and valid pass holders living in Singapore can hire, subject to MOM approval in each case. Expats are generally expected to have family living with them.

**Portal implication:** an employer account is not just an email signup. Serious employers will eventually need Singpass (for MOM’s FDW eService), proof of income, an NRIC-registered address, and EOP completion if they are first-timers. Your onboarding should ask *“first-time employer?”* early, because it changes their timeline.

### 3.3 Things a helper legally cannot do on a Work Permit

These come up constantly in Facebook posts, so the portal’s help content must state them plainly:

- Work for anyone else, at any other address, or in a shop/business — even “part-time cleaning for a neighbour” is illegal deployment.
- Run a business, or work part-time elsewhere.
- Become pregnant or give birth in Singapore, unless married to a Singapore citizen/PR with the Controller of Work Passes’ approval — this rule continues even after the permit becomes invalid.
- Marry a Singapore citizen/PR without prior approval from the Controller of Work Passes.
- Keep working after the permit is cancelled — and she must not start for a *new* employer until the new permit is issued in that employer’s name.

## 4. The hiring process, end to end

MOM publishes this as **“Eight steps to hiring a helper.”** In plain form:

1. **Choose the route** — licensed EA, or direct hire.
2. **Find the right fit** — shortlist, check employment history (an employer with Singpass can check a prospective helper’s employment history in MOM’s FDW eService using her FIN/nationality details), interview in person where possible, otherwise by video/phone.
3. **Prepare before arrival / transfer** — EOP (first-time employers), apply for the Work Permit, get IPA, buy security bond + insurance, set up levy GIRO, arrange medical and SIP, prepare proper accommodation.
4. **Complete the first-week must-dos** — SIP (first-timers, within 7 days of arrival in current MOM guidance — some training centres phrase it as within 3 working days, so book immediately), medical within **14 days** of arrival, apply for Work Permit issuance, fingerprints/photo at MOM Services Centre if required. The card normally arrives within about **5 working days** of registration.
5. **Draft the employment contract and sign the safety agreement** — salary, rest days, duties, termination terms. The safety agreement covers rules like window cleaning.
6. **Set up salary payment** — pay on time and in full, keep records, set up a bank account for the helper (can be requested at Work Permit issuance).
7. **Give clear instructions** — schedule, house rules, phone-use expectations, dietary/cooking needs, medication schedules, emergency contacts.
8. **Check in regularly** — plus formal checks during employment: EA post-placement checks, MOM/CDE interviews for first-time helpers in their first year, 6-monthly medicals, possible house visits.

### 4.1 The three routes are different products

|  | A. Fresh from overseas | B. Ex-Singapore, now overseas | C. Transfer (in Singapore) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where she is | Home country, never worked in SG | Home country, worked in SG before | Singapore, on a current Work Permit |
| How found | Almost always via EA + overseas partner | EA, direct-hire platform, referral | Facebook groups, direct-hire platforms, referrals, EA |
| Extra step | SIP + full arrival process | May be exempt from SIP; overseas-country paperwork still applies (varies a lot by nationality — e.g. Philippines MWO contract verification, Indonesia’s overseas placement process) | **Current employer’s written consent + agreed transfer date are mandatory** |
| Typical timeline | ~4–8 weeks | ~3–6 weeks | ~1–3 weeks once consent and date are agreed |
| Can employer meet her first? | No — video only | No — video only | Yes — in person, usually on her rest day |
| Salary level | Lowest | Middle | Highest |
| Main risk | Unproven in a Singapore home; training need | Overseas paperwork delays | Salary/rest-day expectations are set by the Singapore market; consent or release date can fall through |

**Portal implication:** “Current location” and “status” are not decorative filters. They determine timeline, paperwork, whether an in-person interview is possible, and whether a third party (the current employer) controls the start date. Show **available-from date** and **transfer/release status** on every profile.

### 4.2 Transfer specifics

- New employer submits the Work Permit application **together with the current employer’s written consent**.
- When IPA is ready, **both** employers receive it, and it states the transfer date.
- Do **not** let her start work until the permit is issued in the new employer’s name.
- The current employer should have sent her for her medical; the new employer applies for issuance via FDW eServices.

Facebook posts that say only “transfer helper available” without a release date or consent status are incomplete — this is one of the most common reasons matches collapse in the groups.

## 5. Costs — the full picture

Singapore has **no legislated minimum wage for MDWs**. Salary is negotiated, but source countries set floors that are enforced through their own contract-verification systems, and the Singapore market sets a clear going rate.

### 5.1 Salary — market reality (2026)

Best hard data found: one ethical agency (We Are Caring) published **4,681 placements from Jan 2022 to mid-2026**:

- Average basic salary: **S$880/month in 2025; S$865 in H1 2026**
- By nationality (2022–mid-2026 average): **Filipino S$886, Sri Lankan S$852, Indonesian S$828, Indian S$822, Myanmar S$702**
- Transfer helpers earned about **S$82/month more** than helpers hired from abroad (S$883 vs S$801); transfer average held at **S$911** in H1 2025 and H1 2026
- Prior Singapore experience matters: S$872 vs S$806 for first job in Singapore
- Age/experience matters: age 23–29 averaged S$726; 40–49 averaged S$897; 50+ averaged S$922

Indicative ranges quoted across agencies/platforms in 2026 (basic salary, before rest-day pay and levy):

| Nationality | Fresh / first-timer | Experienced / transfer |
|---|---|---|
| Filipino | S$650–750 | S$750–950+ (S$1,000+ for specialised infant/eldercare) |
| Indonesian | S$550–650 | S$650–800+ |
| Myanmar | S$500–600 | S$600–750+ |
| Sri Lankan / Indian | S$500–650 | S$650–900 |

**Source-country floor to note:** for contracts verified from **21 October 2025**, the Philippine Migrant Workers Office requires at least **US$500 (≈ S$650)/month** for a newly hired Filipino helper, at least 1 full rest day per week, no placement fee charged to her, and no deductions for recruitment/training. A contract below that will not be verified. Indonesia operates its own embassy/placement requirements, which move periodically — do not hard-code a single “minimum by nationality” table into the portal as if it were Singapore law.

**Portal implication:** show *expected salary* as a range the helper sets, and *salary offered* as a range the employer sets. Do not present any figure as “the MOM minimum” — MOM does not set one.

### 5.2 One-time and recurring costs to the employer

| Cost | Amount (2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| MOM Work Permit application | S$35 | Non-refundable |
| MOM Work Permit issuance | S$35 | Total MOM fees S$70 |
| Security bond | S$5,000 | Bank/insurance guarantee, not cash paid out — required for non-Malaysian helpers, must be transmitted to MOM before she enters Singapore |
| Medical insurance | Part of a ~S$400–800 / 26-month insurance package | Minimum **S$60,000/year** for inpatient care and day surgery (raised July 2023) |
| Personal accident insurance | Included above | Minimum **S$60,000** sum assured |
| SIP (first-time helper) | ~S$76–93 | Paid by employer |
| EOP (first-time employer) | ~S$29–76 | Online is cheapest |
| Pre-employment / 6-monthly medical | ~S$50–80 per exam | Paid by employer |
| Airfare (overseas hire / repatriation) | ~S$200–700 | Employer is responsible for return airfare at end of employment |
| Agency service fee (if using an EA) | ~S$1,000–3,000 | No legal cap on what an EA charges *employers* |
| Direct-hire platform fee (if using one) | S$0–~S$100 typical; S$39 is EmployHelpers’ headline price | This is the cost your portal competes on |
| **Monthly: salary** | S$500–1,000+ (see 5.1) | Pay within 7 days after the salary period ends |
| **Monthly: levy** | **S$300** standard; **S$60** concessionary; **S$450** for a second helper | Paid by GIRO. Concession generally if the household has a Singapore-citizen child under 16, a person 67+, or a person with disability needing help with daily activities — check exact criteria in FDW eService |
| Monthly: food, toiletries, upkeep | ~S$120–350 | Adequate food must be provided; do not deduct food/medical costs from salary |

A realistic first-year, all-in figure through an agency is commonly quoted at **~S$1,830–S$4,235 in one-time costs** (excluding the S$5,000 bond guarantee) **plus** salary + levy every month. Direct hire removes most of the agency fee, not the mandatory costs.

### 5.3 What the helper can legally be charged

- EA fee cap for the *worker*: **max 1 month’s salary per year of contract, capped at 2 months’ salary in total** — including fees collected via an overseas agency for the same placement.
- **No fee before placement.** Fees can only be collected after she is successfully placed (for foreigners, after IPA / work visa is obtained).
- Costs incurred in Singapore (e.g. Singapore-side training, medical) should be borne by the employer/EA, not passed to the worker.
- If employment ends **within the first 6 months**, the employer is entitled to a refund of **at least 50% of the placement fees** paid to the EA, subject to conditions (including having notified the EA) — and the EA cannot make a transfer to another employer a condition of that refund.
- The old “placement loan / salary deduction for 6–8 months” model still appears in the market via overseas fees that Singapore cannot fully control. NGOs (HOME, TWC2) document deduction periods stretching well beyond the Singapore cap. A portal that markets itself as fair should adopt a clear rule: **helpers list for free, helpers pay no placement fee on the platform.**

## 6. Ongoing employer obligations (what goes wrong after hiring)

Your portal’s value does not end at the match — most Facebook-group traffic after hiring is about these rules:

- **Rest days:** entitled to **1 rest day per week**, by mutual agreement on which day. Since **1 Jan 2023, at least 1 rest day per month cannot be compensated away** — she must actually take it, even if she offers to work it. Other rest days can be worked only by mutual agreement, paid at **at least 1 day’s salary each** (monthly salary ÷ 26) or replaced by a rest day in the same month. The mandatory day can be taken as a full day or two half-days, at home if she chooses, and can be deferred by at most one month. About 90 employers a year are penalised for breaching the mandatory rest day; the maximum under the Employment of Foreign Manpower Act is S$10,000 fine, 1 year’s jail, or both.
- **Salary:** pay in full and on time — no later than 7 days after the period ends. Bank/GIRO payment is strongly recommended so there is a record. **No unauthorised deductions** (food, medicine, “loan” deductions outside the legal structure).
- **Medical:** pre-employment exam within 14 days of arrival screening for TB, HIV, syphilis and malaria, and certifying fitness for employment. Then **6-monthly medicals**: pregnancy and syphilis every 6 months, HIV every 2 years, TB once after 2 years. Employer pays. If she fails the arrival medical, the permit will not be issued and she must be repatriated.
- **Insurance:** maintain the S$60,000 medical and S$60,000 personal accident cover for the whole employment.
- **Accommodation and food:** proper accommodation — adequate shelter, basic amenities, sufficient ventilation and privacy, a separate sleeping area (not, for example, the same room as a male adult/teenager). Adequate food. Facebook posts advertising “sleeping area in the living room” do appear — flag this in moderation, as it sits poorly with MOM’s accommodation expectations.
- **Safety:** sign the safety agreement; helpers must not clean the outside of windows except under the strict safe conditions in the agreement.
- **Passport:** the employer/agency must **not** retain the helper’s passport without consent-based safekeeping she can retrieve — withholding a passport is one of the most-reported abuses in the groups.
- **Repatriation:** employer pays for the return airfare and related costs when employment ends, cancels the permit within a week of the last working day, and ensures departure within about two weeks.
- **Termination:** either side can terminate under the contract’s notice terms; in practice, helpers who leave early rarely can pay compensation in lieu, and forcing someone to stay creates bigger problems — this is a recurring, emotional theme in employer posts.

## 7. What actually happens in the Facebook groups

*Method note:* the main groups are closed/private, so this section is built from the groups’ public names and descriptions, posts from those groups as reported and quoted by Singapore news sites (The Independent, RedHot Singapore, The Straits Times coverage), public employer job posts mirrored outside Facebook, and the structured profiles on competing portals — which copied the Facebook/biodata format. Treat exact group member counts as unverified; do not quote a count you have not seen inside the group.

### 7.1 The groups that matter

- **Direct Hire Transfer Singapore Maid / Domestic Helper** — the direct-hire/transfer group most often quoted in news coverage. Mix of employer “looking for” posts, helper “looking for employer” posts, and a very large volume of advice/dispute posts.
- **MDW in Singapore** — large general group for employers and helpers; advice, disputes, salary and rest-day debates, plus hiring posts.
- **MDW in Singapore (working conditions forum)** — a variant focused on conditions and disputes.
- Plus many smaller nationality-specific, location-specific, and agency-run groups/pages. Agencies also post in these groups — “no agents” is a phrase employers now add to their posts because of it.

### 7.2 Employer post — the real template

Almost every genuine employer post contains the same fields, in roughly this order:

> *Looking for a transfer helper. Chinese family in [estate], HDB/condo [size]. 2 adults + 2 kids (ages X and Y) [+ elderly mother / dog]. Main duties: childcare / infant care, cooking (Chinese / Indian / Western), general cleaning, marketing. Start date: [date / ASAP]. Looking for: [nationality preference], age [range], English-speaking, finished contract preferred, experience with babies. Salary: S$[X]. Rest days: [2 / 4 Sundays, or 1 day/month + compensation]. Own room / shared room with kids. No agents please. Please PM me / WhatsApp [number].*

Real examples of the detail level (paraphrased from public posts):

- “2 adults + 3 children (2, 8, 10), Central Singapore, 2 days off per month, annual bonus S$120–300, festive bonus, main duties: full care of children including night care for the 2-year-old, cooking, cleaning.”
- “Family of 3 adults, direct hire only — no agents, must currently be in Singapore, minimum 1 completed contract, age 27–40, English, independent household management and Indian (non-veg) cooking, every Sunday off, start December.”

**Fields employers almost always specify:** family composition with children’s/elderly ages, house type and location (estate), start date, nationality preference, language, experience type, the *one main job* the hire is really for, salary, rest days, sleeping arrangement, phone-use expectations, dietary handling (can handle pork / vegetarian household / non-veg cooking).

### 7.3 Helper post — the real template

> *Hello Sir/Madam, I am [name], [age], from [country], [religion], married/single with [N] kids. I am currently in Singapore, finished contract on [date] / can transfer on [date]. I have [X] years experience in Singapore [+ Malaysia / Hong Kong / Middle East / home country]. I can take care of baby/children/elderly, cook [cuisines], do general housework, marketing, pet care. I am honest, hardworking, patient, can work without supervision. Expected salary S$[X], [N] off days. Please PM / WhatsApp me. Thank you.*

This is the Facebook version of the agency biodata (Section 8). Helpers also routinely state **reason for transfer** — employer migrated, finished contract, employer no longer needs a helper, personal/family reasons — because employers treat an unexplained transfer as a risk signal and ask about it in comments.

### 7.4 What each side is really filtering for

**Employers, in order of how often it decides a shortlist:**
1. Status and availability — transfer / finished contract, available-from date, in Singapore now?
2. The main job fit — infant care (newborn experience is its own premium category), childcare by age, eldercare (mobility help, night care, medical/dementia experience), cooking by cuisine, or general housework
3. Nationality + language — English fluency is the usual stated reason for a Filipino preference; Mandarin/Hokkien/Cantonese/dialect ability matters for eldercare in Chinese families; Bahasa/Malay overlap matters in Malay families
4. Singapore experience — years, number of employers, longest stay with one employer
5. Expected salary vs budget, and rest days expected vs offered
6. Age, and — sensitive but universal in biodata — religion, marital status, own children (employers read this as both empathy and flight-risk signal; see the design warning in 11.5)
7. Practical handling: can handle pork, not afraid of dogs / can care for pets, comfortable with cameras in the home, phone-use discipline, willingness to care for a person with disability, strength for transfers/mobility help

**Helpers, in order of how often it decides whether they reply:**
1. Salary and rest days (number, and whether they are Sundays)
2. Workload — number of adults/kids, house size, second helper or solo, night-care duties
3. Sleeping space and food — own room vs shared/living room; whether she eats family food
4. Phone access and freedom on rest days / curfew
5. Family type and location, and whether the work is mainly childcare, eldercare, or cleaning
6. Contract length and start date — and whether the employer will pay for her transfer paperwork or expects her to bear costs (she legally should not bear placement costs)

### 7.5 Recurring friction themes — your help centre and matching questions

These topics repeat so often in the groups that they should become structured fields or pre-interview prompts in the portal, not free-text surprises after hiring:

- **Phone use during work** — the single most common employer complaint post (“on her phone while walking the dogs / missed medication”). Employers who state a clear phone rule upfront report fewer disputes.
- **Rest-day logistics** — meals on rest days, return time/curfew, staying home on a rest day.
- **Experience vs attitude** — “5 years’ experience” used to reject a new family’s way of doing things; employers conflate experience with fit.
- **Cooking mismatch** — helper cooks her own cuisine, family wants theirs; solved when employers teach recipes in the first weeks.
- **Salary fairness inside the house** — second helpers paid more than long-serving first helpers; helpers compare pay in the groups in real time.
- **Early termination cost** — employer has paid agency fees and the helper’s placement loan, helper wants to go home at month 11, replacement window (6 months) has passed, employer bears repatriation. No compensation mechanism exists.
- **Holiday cover** — employer travels, helper is afraid to stay alone / employer does not want her to stay alone or come along; boarding with an agency (~S$30/day is quoted in groups) is the usual workaround.
- **Abuse reports** — passport withheld, inadequate food, scolding/threats of blacklisting, helpers advised to report to MOM rather than run away. The portal needs a visible safety/reporting path (MOM, CDE, HOME) from day one.

## 8. The standard profile: agency biodata

Agencies and portals already converged on a profile format. A portal profile should cover it — minus the fields that should never be public (see 11.5).

**Personal:** first name (full name only after unlock), age, nationality, religion, education level, languages with proficiency (English, Mandarin, Malay, dialect — rated, e.g. learning / average / good / excellent), height/weight (still standard in biodata — include only if you consciously decide to follow industry practice), marital status, number and ages of own children.

**Status:** current location (Singapore / home country / other), status (transfer / finished contract / ex-Singapore / fresh), available-from date, current employer consent status (for transfers), years of total experience, years in Singapore.

**Work history (per employer):** period, country, family composition served, house type/size, duties, salary, reason for leaving.

**Skills (rated, not just ticked):** infant care (0–12 months), childcare, teen care, eldercare, disability care, pet care, cooking by cuisine (Chinese, Indian, Malay/Indonesian, Filipino, Western, vegetarian, halal handling), general housekeeping, laundry/ironing, marketing/groceries, car washing, tutoring/homework supervision, gardening.

**Handling / preferences:** can handle pork, can eat pork, afraid of dogs (yes/no), willing to work with another helper, willing to do night care, phone-use expectation.

**Expectations:** expected salary, expected rest days, preferred start date, self-introduction (“About me” — the free-text paragraph every portal profile leads with), optional short video introduction (NetMaid and others already use this; it noticeably helps employers shortlist).

**Employer (job post) profile — the mirror image:** family composition, ages of children/elderly, house type/size and estate/area, pets, main job and full duty list, work schedule, salary offered, rest days offered, sleeping arrangement, food arrangement, phone rule, start date, nationality/language preferences, experience required, contract length.

Direct arm: full profiles, message her directly, decide at your own pace. |
| **We Are Caring / Ministry of Helpers / HelperFirst etc.** | Licensed EAs with good digital front ends | Employer service fee | The “ethical EA” end: zero/low helper fees, published salary data, post-placement support. They compete on trust, not on being cheapest. A portal that partners rather than fights this tier may close hires faster. |
| **Facebook groups** | Free, unstructured posts + PM/WhatsApp | Nobody | Largest live supply of transfer helpers, worst structure and safety. This is the behaviour you are productising. |

**The gap, stated plainly:** no one combines (a) Facebook-scale live transfer supply, (b) structured, verified profiles with real availability, (c) a fair-to-helpers rule (free for helpers, no placement fee), and (d) a guided, lawful path to actually completing the MOM paperwork. Most platforms do two of the four.

## 10. Portal design — derived from the research

### 10.1 Users and accounts

- **Helper account** — free forever. Phone/WhatsApp-verified. Creates one profile. Can set status: *actively looking / available from [date] / not looking (hidden)*.
- **Employer account** — paid or pay-per-unlock. Identity-verified before contact details are revealed (see 10.4). Creates a household profile and one active job post per intended hire.
- **(Later) Licensed EA partner account** — receives referrals for paperwork/overseas hires, clearly labelled as an EA with its MOM licence number.

### 10.2 Core flow

1. Helper posts profile (guided, in her language where possible — English, Bahasa Indonesia, Burmese at minimum for form help) → moderation + verification → live with available-from date.
2. Employer signs up, builds job post, browses with filters → shortlist → unlock/contact → in-platform chat first, then WhatsApp/video → interview (in person for transfers) → agree salary, rest days, start/transfer date in a written summary inside the platform.
3. **Closing path (choose one, show it honestly):** DIY checklist with MOM links and templates, *or* one-tap referral to a partner licensed EA who does the paperwork for a disclosed fee. The platform does not itself submit the Work Permit application unless it is licensed.
4. After the hire: profile auto-hides, both sides confirm outcome, review/testimonial (previous-employer testimonials are a proven filter — EmployHelpers uses them), and a first-90-days check-in flow (Anisya’s lesson: support after signing is the unmet need).

### 10.3 Filter taxonomy (employer side)

**Hard filters (put these first — they eliminate most profiles):**
Status (transfer / finished contract / ex-Singapore / fresh) · Current location · Available from (date range) · Nationality · Main skill (infant / childcare / eldercare / disability / cooking / housework / pets) · Language (English, Mandarin, Malay + proficiency) · Singapore experience (none / 1–2 yrs / 3–5 yrs / 5+ yrs) · Expected salary (max) · Rest days she expects (max)

**Soft filters (sort / refine):**
Age range · Cuisine experience · Specific experience (newborn, twins, dementia, stroke/mobility, tube feeding — as experience tags, verified only by interview/reference) · Can handle pork · Comfortable with dogs/pets · Willing to share a room with a child/elderly person · Religion (see 11.5) · Has previous-employer testimonial · Has video introduction

**Employer-post filters (helper side — helpers must be able to search jobs too):**
Salary offered (min) · Rest days offered (min) · Family size / number of children · Main job · House type · Area · Own room (yes/no) · Start date

Two-way filtering is not optional. In the groups, helpers choose employers as actively as employers choose helpers, especially experienced transfer helpers.

### 10.4 Verification and trust

- **Helper:** mobile ownership, identity check against the Work Permit record *with her consent* (this is the “MOM-verified” claim competitors make — replicate the check, do not just copy the badge), employment-history consistency, current-employer consent status for transfers (declared, then re-confirmed before paperwork), duplicate/fake profile detection (same photo/phone across profiles).
- **Employer:** mobile + email, identity verification before contact unlock, declaration that they are the actual employer (not an agent), household address area (estate only, never full address publicly).
- **Anti-agent rule:** unlicensed agents posing as employers or helpers are the top complaint in the groups. Require an “I am not an employment agent” declaration, watch for accounts contacting at agent-like volume, and ban accounts that charge helpers.
- **Contact protection:** no public phone numbers (Facebook’s biggest privacy failure). In-platform messaging first; reveal WhatsApp only on mutual unlock. Never display FIN, passport number, full date of birth, or home-country address — to anyone, at any stage, inside the portal.
- **Status hygiene:** profiles expire. Require a tap-to-confirm “still available” every 7–14 days; auto-hide on hire, on expiry, or on report. Stale profiles are what kill trust in the existing portals.

### 10.5 Dignity, sensitive fields, and moderation — design decisions, not decoration

- MOM has prosecuted and suspended agencies for **marketing helpers in an undignified manner** — the 2018 Carousell case, where helpers were advertised like goods on a trading platform, drew a S$20,000 fine for the agent and MOM’s statement that advertising FDWs “on an internet platform meant for trading goods is completely inappropriate and unacceptable” and an offence under the EAA. Language and layout matter legally as well as ethically: no “buy/maid for sale” framing, no price-tag presentation, no browsing grid that treats people as products without context.
- **Religion, marital status, children, height/weight** are standard biodata fields in this industry and employers do ask for them (dietary handling and household fit are the usual reasons given). They are also sensitive personal data. Recommendation: keep religion only where it affects practical fit (e.g. halal handling, pork handling), make sensitive fields optional for the helper to display, and never use them as platform-side ranking signals.
- **Moderation list:** no salary-deduction / loan offers, no passport-holding, no “no off days at all” job posts (illegal since 2023), no living-room sleeping arrangements presented as normal, no requests for part-time/illegal deployment, no contact details in free text, no photos of children in employer posts.
- **Safety surface:** permanent, visible links to MOM reporting, Centre for Domestic Employees (CDE), and HOME, plus a one-tap report on every profile and chat.

### 10.6 Content the portal must ship with

DIY direct-hire checklist (transfer and overseas versions) · EOP/SIP explainers · levy and insurance explainer with current figures · contract template prompts (salary, rest days, duties, phone rule, food, sleeping, termination) · interview question sets by main job (infant, eldercare, cooking) · “questions to agree before signing” checklist built from Section 7.5 · scam warnings.

## 11. The licensing question — read this before building

### 11.1 The law in one paragraph

Under the **Employment Agencies Act**, you need an EA licence from MOM to carry on employment agency activities in Singapore. Operating without one: **fine up to S$80,000, jail up to 2 years, or both** for a first offence (higher for repeat offences). It is also an offence for any person to **engage** an unlicensed EA (fine up to S$5,000 per charge). MOM’s own guidance to workers lists EA-type activities as including: collecting money or benefits for helping someone find a job, **collecting a personal document or resume/CV from a job applicant for the purpose of helping them find a job**, submitting Work Pass applications on behalf of an employer or applicant, and **assisting to place a jobseeker with an employer**.

### 11.2 Why this is genuinely grey for a listing portal

A pure noticeboard — helpers publish their own profiles, employers browse and contact them, the platform charges a flat access/listing fee, staff do **no** shortlisting, recommending, negotiating, or paperwork — is how existing direct-hire platforms argue they are *not* EAs. Every feature that moves you from “noticeboard” toward “we help you find and place the right helper” moves you toward EA activity:

| Feature | Risk direction |
|---|---|
| Self-service profile posting + search filters | Lower — noticeboard-like |
| Flat subscription for employers to view/contact | Lower — this is the EmployHelpers model |
| Charging a fee only when a hire succeeds (“success fee”) | Higher — looks like a placement fee |
| Charging the **helper** anything to list or be placed | Higher — and triggers the fee-cap and no-upfront-fee rules if you are an EA; reputationally toxic regardless |
| Staff/AI shortlisting presented as “we recommend these helpers for you” | Higher — that is placement assistance |
| Collecting resumes/biodata *on behalf of* employers | Higher — named in MOM’s list |
| Submitting Work Permit applications, booking SIP/medical, handling IPA | EA work — do not do this unlicensed |
| Handling money between employer and helper (salary, loans) | Do not do this |

This table is a design guide, not a legal opinion. The boundary is fact-specific, and MOM enforces it.

### 11.3 Your three lawful routes

**Route A — Pure platform (fastest, the competitor-proven path).** Listing + search + messaging + DIY guides. Charge employers only (flat/subscription), helpers free, no success fee, no staff matching, no paperwork submission. Get written legal advice confirming your exact feature set and terms stay outside EA activity, and mirror the disclaimer competitors use.

**Route B — Platform + licensed EA partner (recommended if you want to “complete” hires).** Route A for discovery, plus a disclosed referral to one or more MOM-licensed EAs (verify them in MOM’s EA Directory, show licence numbers, retention and transfer rates) who contract directly with the employer for paperwork, overseas hires, and post-placement support. The partner is the EA; you are the platform. Put the referral fee arrangement in writing and disclose it.

**Route C — Become a licensed EA.** Comprehensive Licence (All) is the only licence type that covers FDW placement. In outline: ACRA-registered business, a Key Appointment Holder who is a Singapore citizen/PR, fit-and-proper (not an undischarged bankrupt, no relevant convictions, not from a revoked EA), **CEI (Certificate of Employment Intermediaries) certification** for the KAH and all EA personnel doing EA work, registration of all EA personnel with MOM, a **security deposit of S$60,000 for a new EA** (banker’s guarantee; scales down with track record/demerit points), licence valid 3 years. This route also brings EA obligations: fee caps, receipts, record-keeping, the 50%-refund rule, demerit points, and MOM’s retention/transfer reporting.

**Recommendation from the research:** launch on Route A, close hires on Route B, and treat Route C as a later decision once volume proves the paperwork revenue is worth the compliance load. Do not launch in the middle — a “platform” that informally does EA work is the worst position.

### 11.4 Other legal/operational notes

- **PDPA:** you will hold sensitive data on a vulnerable population (nationality, religion, health declarations, employment history). Collect the minimum, get explicit consent for each disclosure, let helpers delete/export their profile, set a retention limit, and never sell contact data. A breach here is not a normal SaaS incident.
- **Source-country rules:** Filipino hires involve Philippine MWO contract verification and its no-fee-to-the-worker rule; Indonesian hires involve the Indonesian placement system. A transfer-only launch avoids most of this; an overseas-hire feature does not.
- **Advertising rules:** follow MOM’s dignity standard for all marketing (Section 10.5). Never advertise on goods marketplaces, never use “cheap maid” type copy.
- **No scraping Facebook groups** to seed profiles, and no posting spam in the groups. Seed supply by being useful there within group rules, and by referral from helpers themselves — helpers’ own networks (church, dormitory-day-off gatherings, remittance shops) are how supply actually spreads in this market.

## 12. Business model options (observed, not invented)

| Model | Who does it | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employer flat fee to search/contact (e.g. S$39) | EmployHelpers | Simple, undercuts agencies by ~S$2,000+, easy to explain | Low revenue per hire; needs volume; support costs |
| Employer subscription / contact credits | HelperChoice, HelperPlace | Recurring, matches a search that takes weeks | Churn once hired; pressure to keep profiles looking plentiful (stale-profile trap) |
| Free for both sides + EA referral fee (B2B) | Searchmaid-style hybrids | Aligns with Route B; monetises completion, not browsing | Referral income depends on partner; must disclose |
| B2B listing fees from agencies | NetMaid | Agencies pay reliably | You become an agency channel, not a direct-hire alternative |
| Licensed EA service fee (S$1,000–3,000) | We Are Caring etc. | Highest revenue per placement, includes support | Requires Route C: licence, deposit, CEI staff, compliance |

Pricing anchor to remember: the employer’s alternative is **S$1,500–S$6,000 in agency/placement costs** (figures quoted by direct-hire platforms for the agency route) versus **~S$650 in mandatory costs** to do it themselves. There is clear room for a S$39–S$149 employer product *if* the profiles are real, current, and contactable.

## 13. Decisions needed from you before any build

1. **Launch segment:** transfer / finished-contract helpers only (recommended — fastest, simplest paperwork, exactly the Facebook demand), or include overseas fresh hires from day one (adds EA partners and source-country complexity)?
2. **Licensing route:** A (pure platform), B (platform + EA partner — recommended), or C (get licensed)? This decides the feature list, not the other way round.
3. **Legal review:** approve getting a Singapore EA-law opinion on the exact feature set before development?
4. **Pricing:** flat fee, subscription, or free + referral — and confirm the rule *helpers never pay*.
5. **Verification level at launch:** phone-only, or phone + Work Permit-status check with consent? The badge is your main trust differentiator.
6. **Languages:** English only, or English + Bahasa Indonesia + Burmese for helper onboarding?
7. **Name / positioning:** “direct hire, no agency fees, fair to helpers” is the proven angle (Anisya / EmployHelpers). Confirm this is the positioning you want before branding.
